# OneForge — Creative Brief

*This is not a spec. It's the feeling the site should chase — written so a designer or developer can make their own decisions and still land in the right place. Nothing here should be read as "use this color" or "put this element there"; that's what `design.md` is for. This is the why underneath it.*

---

## The two references, as ideas

**Bethel Sarnia gave us a feeling of ease.** Nothing on that site asks you to work for it. You always seem to know where you are and what happens if you click the thing in front of you. There's no moment where you're holding two decisions at once — the site only ever asks you one thing at a time, and it asks plainly, in words a person would actually say out loud. The idea worth carrying forward isn't any specific layout choice — it's that *confidence looks like restraint*. A visitor should never feel like they're being sold to, routed, or managed. They should feel like they were expected, and the door was already open.

**Pebble Life gave us a feeling of craft.** Scrolling it doesn't feel like reading a brochure — it feels like being told a story by someone who thought carefully about pacing. Nothing is rushed onto the screen at once; each idea gets its own breath before the next one arrives. The color and motion never announce themselves — they just make everything feel considered, like someone cared about the six pixels nobody was supposed to notice. The idea worth carrying forward isn't the specific use of video or gradients — it's that *quality is felt in the pacing and the restraint, not in how much motion is on screen*.

## What OneForge should feel like, sitting between them

Somewhere between a warm front door and a quiet, well-made object. Direct enough that a first-time visitor never has to guess what to do next; considered enough that a returning visitor notices something new about how it feels each time. The site should never feel like a church website template, and it should never feel like a startup's product page either — it should feel like it belongs to no category but its own.

A few honest tensions worth sitting in rather than resolving too quickly:
- **Warm, but not soft.** The brand carries real heat and intensity (fire, forging, pressure) — the site shouldn't sand that down into something purely comforting. There should be moments of real weight.
- **Confident, but never cold.** Directness can tip into corporate blankness if it's not careful — every plain sentence should still sound like a person who's glad you're here.
- **Crafted, but never precious.** The Pebble-style attention to pacing and motion should never slow someone down or get between them and the thing they came to do (finding a service time, hitting play on a message, giving).

## Ideas to carry into the build (not instructions — provocations)

- **The first thing a visitor sees should tell them something true about the tension the whole brand lives in** — not a mission statement, not a stock photo of a smiling crowd. Something that earns a second more of attention.
- **Every action should feel like it costs the visitor nothing to try.** Opening "Listen" or "Plan a Visit" should feel as low-stakes as glancing at something, not like starting a process. If a modal ever feels like a form to get through, that's worth noticing.
- **Motion should feel like punctuation, not decoration.** A good pause, a good reveal, a good transition should land the same way a well-placed comma does — you don't notice it as an effect, you just notice that the sentence reads better.
- **The audio player is a small, personal object.** Someone might have OneForge's teaching playing in the background while they do something else entirely — the floating player should feel like a considerate companion to that, not a chrome widget bolted onto the page. It should feel like it was designed by someone who actually listens to podcasts while doing dishes.
- **Giving should feel like an offering, not a transaction.** Whatever the Give experience ends up looking like, it shouldn't borrow any visual language from checkout flows or donation-guilt patterns common elsewhere online. It should feel more like handing something to a friend than filling out a form.
- **The site can afford a little heat.** Somewhere — maybe just once — let the fire/forge idea show up as something visceral rather than just a metaphor in the copy. Trust that one bold, well-placed moment does more than color everywhere.
- **Let the words take up room.** Confidence reads as size and space, not density — a page that's unafraid to let one idea fill the screen, with air around it, feels like it knows what it's saying. A cramped page feels like it's apologizing for itself.

## What "successful" looks like

A first-time visitor should be able to find a service time in under ten seconds without reading anything they didn't want to read. A returning visitor, six months in, should still notice some small detail they hadn't clocked before. And nobody, at any point, should feel like they're being funneled, upsold, or performed at.

That's the brief. Everything else is craft.
